<Card title="What PreSubmit does">

- Venue compliance: checks the manuscript against a venue profile, a
  requirements URL, or requirements you paste as text.
- Double-blind heuristics — anonymity checks that are an aid, not a compliance
  guarantee.
- Document structure and bibliography checks, plus ORCID and DOI sanity checks.
- Page and abstract limits, driven by the selected venue profile.
- Leftover template text: lorem ipsum, placeholder author and title, stray
  `\todo` notes.
- `\ref` and `\label` integrity — orphan references and duplicate labels.
- Venue profiles for IEEE, ACM, NeurIPS, ICML and ACL, or your own pasted
  requirements.

</Card>

## Why this matters

Most desk rejections are avoidable formatting and completeness problems, not the
science. PreSubmit is a final readiness pass: it returns a prioritized issue
list — missing required sections, missing title or abstract, duplicated
sentences, overlong sentences, bibliography problems — so you fix the mechanical
issues before a reviewer ever sees them.

Issues are sorted by severity, errors first, then warnings, then information,
and each one can be marked fixed or ignored as you work through them.

## Examples

**Prioritized issue list.** On a draft missing its front matter, PreSubmit
returns findings such as "missing required section", "no title detected" and "no
authors detected", grouped by area so you work top-down.

## Questions people ask

**What can I bring?** LaTeX source and the common manuscript formats. A scanned
or image-only PDF with no extractable text is detected and reported rather than
silently passed.

**Is my manuscript stored?** No manuscript text is kept after the check
completes.

**Does it guarantee acceptance?** No. It catches mechanical and completeness
issues, not the quality or novelty of your research.

**Can it check venue-specific rules?** Supply a venue's requirements and
PreSubmit checks against them; without one it applies general
submission-readiness checks.
