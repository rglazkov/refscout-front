<Card title="What BibCheck does">

- Flags duplicate keys and matching titles, broken cite keys, and missing
  references.
- Missing and orphan citation checks against your manuscript text: entries you
  never cite, and citations with no `.bib` entry.
- Optional verification against live scholarly databases — roughly a second per
  reference, about a minute for a large bibliography.
- Retraction detection: flags a cited work that Crossref reports as retracted.

</Card>

## Why this matters

A single duplicate key, or an entry you cite but forgot to define, can bounce a
submission or silently drop a reference. BibCheck reads your `.bib` and your
manuscript and returns a line-by-line problem report: duplicate keys and
matching titles, broken cite keys, entries you never cite, and citations with no
matching entry. Verification against live databases catches the references that
do not resolve at all.

## Examples

**Duplicate detection.** Two entries, `smith2020` and `smith20`, share the DOI
`10.1/x` — BibCheck flags `smith20` as a duplicate of `smith2020`, with the
match type and score.

**Orphan check.** Your `.bib` defines `jones2019` but the manuscript never cites
it — reported as an uncited entry, so you can drop it before submission.

## Questions people ask

**Do I need the manuscript, or just the .bib?** The `.bib` alone covers
duplicates, broken keys and malformed entries. Add the manuscript to also get
missing-citation and orphan checks.

**What does verification against databases do?** It checks your entries against
live scholarly databases and adds up to about a minute for a large
bibliography. It flags entries that do not resolve; it does not rewrite them.

**Is my bibliography stored?** No manuscript or bibliography text is kept after
the check completes.

**Does it fix problems automatically?** No. BibCheck reports issues so you stay
in control of the edits; it does not silently change your file.
